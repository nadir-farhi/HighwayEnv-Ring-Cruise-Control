About this simulation

The cars follow HighwayEnv's IDMVehicle rules, rewritten in JavaScript: IDM car following, MOBIL lane changes checked every second, HighwayEnv's lateral position and heading controller, and the kinematic bicycle model on the curved ring (radius L/2π). The middle lane carries the m sections of length l.

  • Hard speed cap: the IDM acceleration is limited so that the speed never exceeds the set speed; MOBIL's predictions include the cap. This is the cap of the CC model.
  • Speed limit: HighwayEnv's lane.speed_limit, which lowers the IDM desired speed. Because the IDM approaches its desired speed only asymptotically, cars then cruise below the limit.
  • Measured v̄: the mean speed over the last third of the run without control (on the Stern et al. ring: the mean speed over the minute centred on the wave onset); it is the set speed of the hard cap, the speed limit and FollowerStopper. Equilibrium ve: the speed of the uniform IDM equilibrium at the mean spacing of the ring.
  • Stern et al. ring: 260 m, one lane, 22 cars by default, as in the field experiments of Stern et al. (2018). The human IDM was fitted to the speed spread and throughput of the uncontrolled phase of those experiments, and a small random acceleration noise triggers the waves. The control is switched on when the speed spread first exceeds 2.5 m/s, the wave-onset threshold of the experiments.
  • FollowerStopper (Stern et al. 2018): the commanded speed is 0, the leader speed, or the set speed, depending on the bumper gap compared with three thresholds 4.5, 5.25 and 6 m plus a braking margin of 1.5, 1 and 0.5 m/s²; acceleration = (commanded − actual speed)/time step.
  • PI with saturation (Stern et al. 2018): target = own average speed over 38 s plus up to 1 m/s when the gap exceeds 7 m (full at 30 m), bounded by the desired speed; blended with the leader speed when the gap is short.
  • ACC (Milanés & Shladover 2014): a = 0.23 (gap − 2 m − 1.1 s × speed) + 0.07 (leader speed − speed) within 120 m, at most 0.4 (cruise speed − speed).
  • FollowerStopper, PI and ACC cars keep their lane and share an emergency-braking fallback. Equipped cars are spread evenly in each lane. Human drivers predict equipped cars with the IDM in their lane-change decisions, as in HighwayEnv.
  • As in the Python study, cars cannot reverse, and a car that has decided to change lane is visible to the vehicles of its target lane. Each car starts at the IDM equilibrium speed for its gap to the car ahead (at rest in the block).
  • Checked against HighwayEnv 1.12 in Python on the 3-lane ring of 30 × 200 m: block without CC 24.6 km/h and 986 veh/h per lane in both; with the speed limit 20.0 km/h and 799 veh/h; with the hard cap 24.6 km/h and 986 veh/h.
